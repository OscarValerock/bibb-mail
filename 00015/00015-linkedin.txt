OSCAR'S TAKE
KPI blind spots and your new bibb account

Almost 20 years after graduating with my bachelor's, I enrolled in an MSc in Business Information Systems at FHNW this year. I am enjoying the school a lot, and one of the first things that constantly surprises me is how much of what I learned by doing already has a name in academic literature.

In my Business Intelligence course, I came across the concept of formula incompleteness, which I was already very familiar with in practice. The idea is simple: a KPI is built to answer one question, and over time, that question becomes the only question anyone asks.

The problem is that goals often have several dimensions, but formulas often do not. This doesn't mean the metric is wrong; it's just incomplete. Incomplete, in practice, means misleading.

I have a clear example from a client engagement: they implemented a corporate-wide offshoring project, and the project's success was measured by a single metric: the offshore-to-total-headcount ratio. This felt appropriate, and most quarters, the metric was in the green.

What the report did not show was the rotation rate: contractors were churning at 68% per year, and each replacement triggered a full onboarding cycle. The estimated cost was CHF 240K per year. Invisible, because nobody had built the measure. The data existed. The question had not been asked.

In this week's post, I walk you through the client case and three DAX measures you can apply today.
