### Day -2 — Standalone insight (no newsletter mention)

Can we connect Power BI directly to Salesforce?

Tthat is a question I've been often asked and the answer is yes, but with some caveats: the native Salesforce Reports connector caps at 2,000 rows. Most teams discover this after they have already built reports that depend on it.

This is not a Salesforce problem specifically. It is a native connector problem as many of this connector come with rate limits, missing fields, or refresh flows that break under real workloads. Enterprise-grade reporting is rarely viable through these connectors out of the box.

The fix is almost always the same: move the data out of the SaaS tool and into something you control before Power BI touches it. Traditionally, that means a pipeline, which translates to more infrastructure. That means a decision most teams do not know they need to make until it is too late.

Anton Storozhuk, Founder of Metrica Software, wrote the clearest breakdown of this I have read, specifically for the Salesforce case. Three approaches, real trade-offs, no marketing language.

### Day +2 — Follow-up insight

One line from Anton Storozhuk's Salesforce Power BI article has stayed with me:

"The real decision is whether data is shaped once and reused, or repeatedly rebuilt across reports."

That applies beyond Salesforce. Any time you pull data from a source system into a BI tool, you're making a choice about where the definition lives. In the report. In the pipeline. In the tool.

Most teams don't make that choice explicitly. They inherit it from whoever built the first dashboard.

Anton's guide is the clearest breakdown of this for the Salesforce case. Link in comments.
